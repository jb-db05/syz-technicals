## Trend rule in force (adopted 22 Nov 2026 – BUILD_SPEC §2.2 / §3.2, code `syztech/trend/swing_levels.py`)
- **Hierarchy (24 Nov 2026)**: (1) the 200-day slope sets the regime and caps the verdict on its side – a falling 200-day is never Bullish; (2) price structure confirms or denies – a denial alone gives Neutral with a tilt; (3) the 50-day vs the 200-day arbitrates – going against the regime needs both a structure break and the 50-day on the wrong side. One signal never overturns the slope, two confirmations do; the override works downward only. Full slope dominance was rejected (the 200-day lags after parabolas).
- **Direction**: 200-day average over 20 trading days – rising / flat / falling (0.20% band). Unchanged.
- **Support / resistance**, per side, in priority order: (1) Florian's drawn levels (text, then chart); (2) **swing levels** – last confirmed swing low / swing high; (3) 52-week low / high only where no swing is confirmed yet or the series has values ≤ 0 (negative yields).
- **Swing**: confirmed when the close reverses **k = 1.5 × the median one-month move of the past year** from the extreme (one-month move = stdev of daily log returns over 100 days × √20; median over 252 bars – robust threshold adopted 23 Nov 2026 so a parabola or crash cannot inflate it). Closes only, nothing filled.
- **A broken level stands**: after a lower low, the broken prior swing low stays the support until the resistance breaks; after a higher high, the prior swing high stays the resistance. Swing-level breaks count from the earlier of the two swing points; drawn levels keep their `levels_asof − 100 bars` window.
- **Verdict table (§3.3)**: rising + support intact + **50-day above the 200-day** = Bullish (variant B, 23 Nov 2026 – with the 50-day below it: Neutral+); falling + support broken = Bearish; in between Neutral with a tilt (Neutral+ / Neutral−), plus the death-cross and losing-momentum cases.
- Levels with a `52w` or `swing` origin in a universe file are recomputed at every run, never treated as drawn. Charts label them `swing low / swing high` (grey dashed); the trigger watch uses them.
- Basis: side-by-side study (`syztech/research/`, 436 series 2019–2026): robust k = 1.5 matches 68 / 80 published calls (drawn levels 67, 52-week 57), 14 / 17 Bearish calls caught, 2.85 verdict changes a year; variant B keeps 68 / 80 and 14 / 17 at 2.96 changes a year. No rule separated forward 1–3-month returns – the verdict describes the trend, it does not forecast.
- **Relative trend (Florian's second decision of 24 Nov 2026 – replaces the three-state version adopted earlier that day)**: kept simple – only the slope of the 200-day average of the relative-strength line (price ÷ benchmark × 100), measured over 20 trading days. Rising = **Outperform**, falling = **Underperform**; exactly unchanged = Neutral (no flat band, in practice never). No price structure, no 50-day. Needs more than 220 common closes. Code `score.relative_verdict` → `rel_state`, `rel_rule` (`rs_ma200_rising` / `rs_ma200_falling`). Same signal as the former RS Mom + / −, shown as ▲ Outperform / ▼ Underperform; RS 3m stays as the early-warning number. Informational: it never changes the absolute verdict. The 24 Nov version (full trend rule on the RS line, three states) is withdrawn; its study stays in `syztech/research/study_rs.py` (slope-only: 1.67 changes a year, 13-week forward relative spread 0.58 pt). Weekly cross-asset and Swiss list documents still print RS Mom + / − (same signal).
- **Charts, relative-strength panel (24 Nov 2026)**: the RS line is blue (`#2F6FDE`) while its 200-day average is rising and red (`#D8343F`) while it is falling, bar by bar; grey before the average exists; the 200-day of RS is a thin navy line. These two colours are outside the Syz palette, at Florian's request.
- Never change the trend rule or k without a new study and Florian's decision.

## Thematic Technical Review (started 24 Nov 2026 – draft, HTML only so far)
- Data: `data/prices_thematics`, `data/universe_thematics.csv` (315 lines + 14 factor series; SARK US and NUCL US removed; BION SW and YCA LN kept as the biotech and uranium proxies). Everything in USD, dividend-adjusted.
- **Themes and anchors (Florian, 24 Nov 2026)**: only proper themes are clustered and shortlisted. Sectors, markets / countries / size, styles / factors, commodities and crypto are **anchors** – read with the same engine, shown in their own section, never clustered, never on the shortlist. Sectors are anchors without exception. The type of each line is in `syztech/report/assets/thematics_types.csv` (my classification; Florian may correct it).
- Clustering (`syztech/research/theme_clusters.py`): three years of weekly returns in USD, MSCI ACWI beta removed, average-linkage clustering on the residual correlations. Fine map (cut 0.55, names in `theme_cluster_page.NAMES`, confirmed) is kept as the sub-group label; the review clusters the themes alone at a looser cut (0.70, `thematic.THEME_CUT`) – then clean energy and batteries / metals / miners are split by hand at Florian's request (`thematic.SPLITS`): 12 clusters + 3 stand-alone themes; names in `thematic.TOP_NAMES` are proposals. Removing the dollar was tested and not adopted.
- Duplicates: one line per set of series with residual correlation ≥ 0.95, or the same ticker root on two exchanges (≥ 0.80) – a fund before an index, then the longest history.
- Review (`syztech/report/thematic.py`, page `thematic_html.py`): each theme and each equal-weight cluster composite is read with the trend engine in force and the relative trend against the MSCI ACWI. Momentum score (ranking only): average over 3 / 6 / 12 months of return minus ACWI, divided by own volatility. Shortlist = Bullish + rising 200-day + Outperform, at most 2 per cluster; watch list = Neutral + rising + Outperform.
- The momentum ranking was tested on the cluster composites (monthly, Feb 2020 – Oct 2026): on the theme clusters, top third minus bottom third about +0.1% over one month and +1.2% over three (t about 1.4) – not significant. It orders the shortlist; it is never presented as a forecast.
- **Portfolio test** (`syztech/research/theme_backtest.py` → `output/thematics/backtest.json`, shown on the page; about three minutes, re-run by hand when data change): the shortlist rule rebuilt every 21 trading days, May 2020 – Oct 2026, 81 periods, equal weights, no costs. Shortlist rule 18.3% a year (vol 18.9%, worst drawdown −18.7%) vs MSCI ACWI 13.9% and all themes equal-weight 15.9% (drawdown −31.2%); excess +4.7% a year vs ACWI (t 1.0) and +2.4% vs all themes (t 0.7) – not significant; +8.3% in the first half of the test, +1.2% in the second. Variants: cluster confirmation and RS 3m > 0 did not help; holding until the theme weakens (verdict Neutral− / Bearish or Underperform) kept the return and cut monthly turnover from 35% to 16%; one per cluster risk-weighted similar. Caveats to repeat with any use: seven years, clusters formed on 2023–26 data, only funds that exist today, no costs.
- **Six-monthly rebalancing with a stop on Bearish** (Florian's request, same module, 7-day grid, average of the six possible starting months, Apr 2020 – Nov 2026): no stop 12.7% a year (+1.0% vs ACWI, against +3.4% for monthly rebalancing on the same grid); stop with proceeds in the ACWI 12.2% (+0.5%), in cash 12.0% (+0.3%); about 7 stops a year; worst drawdown not reduced (−30.5% vs −29.5%). The Bearish verdict comes late (falling 200-day and broken support), so the stop sells near lows. Not adopted as a rule; no rule was changed.
- **Yearly rebalancing with a stop on Bearish** (same test, 12 starting months): no stop 8.6% a year (−1.9% vs ACWI); stop with proceeds in the ACWI 8.8% (−1.6%), in cash 8.2% (−2.3%, average worst drawdown −24.7% vs −29.4%); about 8 stops a year; return from 4.1% to 13.6% depending on the starting month. A yearly schedule loses the rule's advantage entirely. Not adopted.
- **The page shows no back-test (Florian, 25 Nov 2026)**: the portfolio tests and the ranking test stay in `syztech/research/` and in this file; they are not sections of the Thematic Technical Review.

- **Weekly rule, this document only (Florian, 25 Nov 2026)**: `syztech/trend/weekly.py` – the rule in force with every window ÷ 5 (40-week and 10-week averages, direction over 4 weeks with the same 0.20% band, 20-week band, swing = 1.5 × median one-month move measured on weekly closes, 64 weeks minimum; relative trend = direction of the 40-week average of price ÷ benchmark over 4 weeks). - Screen tools (26 Nov 2026): Reset; Copy Bloomberg tickers (the filtered list, one ticker per line); a star at the right of each row flags a company into **My short list** (own section and table, also a filter and a button in the chart window; kept in the browser's local storage, with copy and clear / undo). ## Style conventions (outputs, charts, text)
- Nunito Sans everywhere; Syz colours: Mint `#3BAF90` bullish, Blueberry `#4B5F80` neutral, Mulberry `#AC5D85` bearish, orange `#FF6C0E → #FFA400`, navy `#202945`.
- En dashes only (U+2013). Decimals: 1 for equities/commodities/crypto, 2 for FX and yields, 4 where `universe.csv` says so.
- Chart verdict badges: only the three reference badges in `syztech/charts/assets/verdict_badges_reference.pptx` (19 Nov 2026) – ▲ BULLISH on Mint, ◆ NEUTRAL on Blueberry, ▼ BEARISH on Mulberry; rounded 1.18 × 0.34 in, white bold capitals, letter-spaced. Drawn only by `syz_style.draw_verdict_badge`. No other badge text (no tilt, no "losing momentum", no subtitle inside the badge): Neutral+/Neutral- show NEUTRAL, Bearish_LM shows BEARISH; the shade stays in tables.
- Sectors & Style tables (24 Nov 2026): no `Last` column; the 200-day direction and the relative trend are shown as badges like the verdict – ▲ Rising / ◆ Flat / ▼ Falling and ▲ Outperform / ◆ Neutral / ▼ Underperform, white bold on Mint / Blueberry / Mulberry. The global sector map is kept clean (badge only, no second line): page 1 splits each sector × region cell in two – momentum (200-day direction) left, relative trend right; page 2 is the verdict. The HTML switch offers Momentum | Relative trend (default), Momentum, Relative trend, Verdict. In the split map the region name sits over the momentum half and `vs <benchmark>` over the relative half; every separation (between halves, between regions, in the header) is the same 1.5 pt white line. Evolution (24 Nov 2026): the same rules are re-run on the data to 3, 6 and 9 months before the last close – one page of counts per region (rising 200-day, Outperform, Bullish at 9m / 6m / 3m / now), one page with the split map three months ago; in the HTML a date switch (Today / 3 / 6 / 9 months ago) redraws the map; no counts table there (removed 24 Nov 2026). The style & size table uses the same split layout (24 Nov 2026): one row per kind of ratio (`sectorstyle.STYLE_ROWS`), one column per region, left half = direction of the ratio's 200-day, right half = ratio trend (verdict); grey dash where a region has no such ratio. Sector reports (S&P 500 IT, Swiss) still use the earlier table layout.
- Rotation maps (24 Nov 2026): the dot is today's actual position; the trail is the last 20 trading days, smoothed (anchors every 4 bars, each the average of the daily points within 5 bars either side, the window closing on the last bar) and drawn as a curve that thins and fades towards the past. No intermediate dots. Labels are placed to avoid each other. Same drawing in the PowerPoint (`sectorstyle.rotation_map`) and the HTML.
- Chart source line: `Sources : Syz Research, Bloomberg`. Never name any external research provider in any output.
- Reports state facts and the rule that fired; no interpretation prose – the analyst writes that.
